A cruise stop in Rio de Janeiro is a short, exciting day: a lot to see, a ship that will not wait. The goal is to see the best of the city **and** return to the port calmly. That comes down to planning.

## Start from your all-aboard time

Do not begin with the sights. Begin with the **time you must be back on board**, and work backwards. Check the arrival time, the all-aboard time and how far your pier is from the places you want to visit. A good plan leaves a **buffer**, because traffic in Rio can change quickly.

## What fits in a day in port

With a full day, the classic highlights are realistic:

1. **Christ the Redeemer** early, while the day is fresh.
2. **The Selarón Steps** and a quick look at the city center.
3. **Sugarloaf Mountain** if time and light allow.
4. **A beach stop** at Copacabana or Ipanema if you have a few minutes left.

With less time, it is better to do **fewer things well** than to rush through all of them. The [Rio Express tour](/tours/rio-express) can be shortened around your schedule.

## Ship excursion or private guide?

Ship excursions are convenient and run on the ship's timetable, but they are group experiences with fixed stops. A **private guide** adapts the day to you: more time where you want it, less where you do not, and a route built around your ship's schedule rather than a generic one. You are in a small group of your own, never mixed with strangers.

## Before you go

- Confirm your **arrival and all-aboard times** on the ship's app or itinerary.
- Carry your **ID or a copy of your passport** and your **ship card**.
- Wear comfortable shoes, sunscreen and a hat, and bring water.
- Leave valuables you do not need on the ship.
