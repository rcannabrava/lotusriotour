"Is Rio safe?" is the first question almost every traveler asks me. The honest answer has two parts: **yes, most visitors enjoy Rio without any problem**, and **no, it is not a city where you switch your attention off**. Like any large city, it rewards a little preparation.

## What to expect

The risk visitors meet most often is **petty theft**: a phone taken from a hand, a bag left unattended on the beach. These are opportunity crimes. They are far less likely when you do not look like an easy opportunity, and most of the advice below is about exactly that.

## Simple habits that make a difference

- **Keep your phone out of sight** in crowded streets and on the sidewalk by the road. Take it out to photograph, then put it away.
- **Leave what you do not need at the hotel**: passport (carry a copy), extra cards, expensive jewelry and watches.
- **Carry only what you need** for the day, in a small bag you keep in front of you.
- **At the beach**, bring little, never leave your things alone and avoid empty stretches.
- **At night**, use a taxi or a ride app instead of walking long distances, and stay in busy, well-lit areas.
- **Ask before you go.** Your hotel or your guide knows which areas are fine at which hours.

## Where visitors usually go

Areas such as **Copacabana, Ipanema, Leblon and the main attractions** are visited by thousands of people every day and are comfortable during the day. The key is the same everywhere: awareness, not fear.
